## Board and party games

- Mainstream party games rely on bought pieces and mostly English rules. Evidence for this in the Philippines is the PRD problem statement, not a survey.
- Jackbox sells packs from USD 24.99 [S4]. Core loop is a shared screen with personal devices. Assumption, verify on the Jackbox site.
- DeskTop differs by using what is already on the table and by running a persistent campaign across nights.
- Risk: a free card or party game is "good enough" for casual groups. DeskTop must win on novelty and local flavor.

## Filipino-themed tabletop games

- Lagim is a card game of strategy and luck about Filipino folklore monsters, and it aims to promote original Filipino content [S7].
- Engkanto is a Filipino mythology RPG board game where players act as Bayani seeking Biringan City [S8].
- Tadhana is an RPG set in the Kalawakan universe, inspired by folklore, urban legends and mythology [S9].
- Takeaway: local designers already treat folklore as a selling point. DeskTop is not first on theme, so lead with the physical-object and voice interaction.
- Takeaway: these are bought objects. DeskTop uses objects players already own.
- Possible collaboration path: none contacted. Do not claim any relationship.

## AR tabletop and mixed reality

- Tilt Five uses AR glasses and a physical board, priced at USD 359 for a single-glasses kit [S1].
- Osmo uses a clip-on mirror and stand so an iPad camera sees physical pieces and hands [S3].
- Osmo partnered with Mattel on MindRacers using real Hot Wheels cars [S3].
- Lego AR and other Mattel AR experiments were searched but not verified here. Open question.
- Takeaway: closest in spirit is Osmo, because both read real objects through a camera. Differences are the tablet, the proprietary pieces, the kids focus and no voice or AI game master (Assumption for the last two, verify).
- Takeaway: DeskTop avoids the USD 359 hardware barrier [S1].

## Webcam and phone party games

- Jackbox is the benchmark for same-room group play [S4].
- No webcam-driven party game found that reads arbitrary desk objects. Absence of evidence, not proof, so re-check before claiming "first".
- Takeaway: DeskTop claims "uses a webcam to see your own things", not "first webcam game".

## AI game masters and storytelling

- Friends and Fables offers an AI game master with plans at USD 19.95, 29.95 and 39.95 per month [S5].
- AI Dungeon sells memberships from USD 9.99 to 49.99 per month [S6].
- Both are screen-and-text experiences. Neither is verified to read the physical table, listen to a room or localize to Filipino folklore.
- Takeaway: DeskTop's AI GM narrates only. Deterministic code decides combat (PRD). Say so, because it answers the "AI makes it up" concern.

## Physical-to-digital object recognition

- Osmo's Reflective AI is the clearest precedent for recognizing physical objects [S3].
- DeskTop recognizes unknown everyday objects rather than a fixed piece set, then redraws them as pixel sprites. This is the novelty claim, to be proven by object registration tests (PRD success metric: 9 of 10).
- Takeaway: a fixed piece set is more reliable, so DeskTop must show reliable registration live.

## Filipino folklore video games

- Good Knight features tikbalang, kapre and aswang [S10].
- Agnostiko ORIGINS is a Metroidvania with aswang, kapre, tikbalang, manananggal, duwende and white lady [S12].
- Other titles in search results: Taguan, Nightfall, Aswang, Balete City and Tales of the Aswang VR [S11].
- Takeaway: single-player digital games with folklore monsters exist. The same-table, hands-on format is not seen in these results.
- Takeaway: do not claim to be the first folklore game. Claim a different way to play.

## Implications for DeskTop

- Impact framing: the white space is the combination, not any single feature. Present the matrix, not a "first" claim.
- Impact framing: folklore is already validated by Filipino designers [S7] [S8] [S9] [S10], so frame DeskTop as extending that tradition into object-based play.
- Demo: show an ordinary mug and coin becoming sprites, a Taglish order moving a unit, then the GM narration. This hits three matrix columns in 30 seconds.
- Demo: show the cost line (about USD 0.50 per night) against USD 359 hardware [S1] and USD 20+ per month AI subscriptions [S5].
- Launch: position against Jackbox and board game nights as "plays with what's on your desk", not against AI Dungeon.
- Risk: if judges know Osmo, pre-empt with the difference in pieces, voice and local setting.
